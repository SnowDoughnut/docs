[Pacing](/glossary#pacing) is a weekly check with a monthly consequence. A campaign that under-delivers by 8% a week finishes 30% under budget, and nobody notices until the flight is over and the money is gone unspent.

This runs against the daily rate set in [budget planning](/budget-planning).

## The one calculation

**Pace = spend to date ÷ (daily rate × days elapsed)**

- **1.0** is exactly on plan.
- **Above 1.0** is running hot and will exhaust the budget early.
- **Below 1.0** is underspending and will leave money unspent at the end.

_E.g. Day 20 of a 50-day flight at \$500 a day. Expected spend is \$10,000. Actual is \$8,400. Pace is 0.84, meaning the campaign is tracking to finish around \$21,000 of a \$25,000 budget._

## When to act

Not every variance is a problem. Acting on noise is how a campaign gets reset out of its learning phase every week.

| Pace | Week 1 | Week 2 onward |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Above 1.25 | Expected, platforms front-load | Act |
| 1.10 to 1.25 | Watch | Watch, act if it holds two weeks |
| 0.90 to 1.10 | On plan | On plan |
| 0.75 to 0.90 | Watch | Act |
| Below 0.75 | Investigate, usually a delivery problem | Act |

<Warning>
  Week 1 is not comparable to any other week. Platforms spend aggressively while learning, and most campaigns pace above 1.2 for the first few days. Reacting to that by cutting budgets is the most common self-inflicted pacing wound.
</Warning>

## Underspending is a delivery problem first

The instinct is to raise budgets. Check the cause first, because raising the budget on a campaign that can't spend what it already has does nothing.

| Likely cause | Check |
| :-- | :-- |
| Audience too small | Platform's estimated reach against your delivery |
| Bid or cost cap too low | Whether the platform reports losing auctions on price |
| Too many ad sets splitting the budget | See [campaign structure best practices](/campaign-structure-best-practices) |
| Ads rejected or in limited status | Platform's review state, per ad |
| Schedule restricting delivery | Dayparting or flight dates set narrower than intended |

## What to change, in order of preference

<Steps>
  <Step title="1. Fix delivery">
    Widen the audience, resolve rejections, consolidate ad sets. This is the only intervention that adds volume rather than moving it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="2. Shift between channels">
    Move budget from a channel underdelivering on cost per result to one that isn't. Record the shift and the reason, because it will explain a movement in next week's report.
  </Step>
  <Step title="3. Adjust the daily rate">
    Recalculate against the remaining days, not the original flight. Raising the daily rate late in a flight buys less than the arithmetic suggests, since the platform still needs time to spend it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="4. Release the reserve">
    Only if the release condition set in [budget planning](/budget-planning) is met. A reserve released because a campaign is underspending, rather than because it is working, is just a larger loss.
  </Step>
  <Step title="5. Accept the underspend">
    A legitimate outcome. A campaign that cannot spend efficiently should not spend. Record it so the retrospective can ask why the plan assumed it could.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Record every change

Any budget change explains a movement in a later report. Undocumented changes are why a report says performance improved when what actually happened is that someone moved \$4,000.

Log the date, what changed, by how much, who decided, and why. The [campaign QA checklist](/campaign-qa-checklist) checks this weekly, and [budget reporting](/budget-reporting-process) reads from it.

## Exceptions

Always-on budgets pace against a monthly rate rather than a flight. The calculation is the same with the month as the denominator, and the month-end cliff is the thing to watch: a campaign that paces at 1.15 all month stops delivering in the last four days.
