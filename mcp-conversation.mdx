Working with the MCP reads like working with a colleague who knows the framework. Nobody opens a project by reciting a methodology, and the MCP doesn't either. It asks what it needs, one short question at a time, and the frameworks stay in the background, where it reads them for you.

## The contract

Every [command](/mcp-commands) and [skill](/skills-overview) follows the same rules.

1. **One question per turn.** A question is one or two sentences. When several inputs are missing, they arrive as a short sequence of turns rather than one long form.
2. **Options come from the framework's own lists, best first.** When an answer has a closed list behind it (hook types, copy frameworks, campaign types, dictionary values), the question offers the five options that best fit the work, each with a short reason, and ends with "or see all". Ranking uses each option's Best for, described in the [registry](/mcp-registry#how-options-get-ranked). Typing is for the answers only you hold.
3. **Known answers are never asked.** Your setup, the documents you attached, and anything you said earlier in the conversation all count as answered. An intake with nothing missing is skipped whole.
4. **Confirm in one line, then produce.** Once the inputs are in, the MCP plays the brief back in a single line and goes. It does not restate the whole brief at essay length.
5. **The work lands in your tools.** The output goes where your setup says finished work lives. Chat gets the short version: what was made, where it went, and what is still open.
6. **The frameworks are read, never recited.** A skill loads the copy framework, the hook list, and the template itself. You see their effect in the work, and you see the material only when you ask for it.

## What it sounds like

A Doughnut Labs marketer asks for ad copy:

> **You:** I need help writing some Meta copy.
>
> **MCP:** What ad format is it for? Static image, carousel, or video.
>
> **You:** Static image.
>
> **MCP:** Attach the image, or point me at it in your creative library.
>
> **You:** _(attaches it)_
>
> **MCP:** Which hook type? For a spring sale to warm audiences: FOMO, scarcity, urgency, value, or risk reversal. Or see all ten.
>
> **You:** FOMO.
>
> **MCP:** Which copy framework? Four-part, or problem and solution.
>
> **You:** Four-part.
>
> **MCP:** What's the landing page?
>
> **You:** doughnutlabs.com/spring
>
> **MCP:** Static image, FOMO hook, four-part framework, landing on the spring page. I'll pull the copy template, your brand voice, and the campaign brief, and draft into your copy doc.

Six short turns. The campaign brief already named the angle, so that question never came up. The marketer typed one URL and picked three options. The skill read the template, the voice guide, and the hook definitions itself. That intake is the [Meta ad copy skill](/skill-meta-ad-copy)'s runbook, and the lists behind the questions are [hook types](/hook-types) and [copywriting frameworks](/copywriting-frameworks).

## Wizards

A wizard is any flow longer than a few questions: setup, filling in a dictionary, building a brief from scratch. Every wizard runs the same way.

- **A header on every turn.** The first line names the wizard, the section, and the step, so you always know where you are: **Snow Doughnut setup · Dictionaries · 4 of 9**.
- **Each section opens with its purpose.** One or two sentences on what the section sets up and why it matters, with a link to the page that explains it. Until setup is done, links go to docs.snowdoughnut.com; after that, they go to your own copy, wherever your setup record says it lives.
- **Recommended or advanced first.** A wizard opens by offering the recommended path, which applies the framework's full defaults in one step for you to review, or the advanced path, which asks section by section.
- **A recommended answer on every question.** For a list question it is usually "add all of them", marked recommended and offered first.
- **Every value can be changed.** Offered values are a starting point. Say so in the question: rename `tC` to `tCPA`, drop a row, or add one that isn't listed.
- **Progress saves as it goes**, to your setup record, so stopping halfway loses nothing.

What a wizard turn looks like:

> **Snow Doughnut setup · Dictionaries · 4 of 9**
>
> Bid types record how each campaign was told to optimize, so a report can compare like with like. [Campaign naming: bid type](/campaign-naming-conventions#bid-type)
>
> Add all five (recommended): tROAS (`tR`), tCPA (`tC`), Maximize conversions (`MAXC`), Highest volume (`HV`), Manual CPC (`MCPC`). Rename any of them, for example `tC` to `tCPA`, or pick only the ones you use.

## Ground rules

- **Location comes first.** With no setup record linked, the first question is where to save. Nothing else runs until there is somewhere to write.
- **Never assume new or existing.** A team can have brand files already and no naming conventions yet. Ask per section whether a document exists, and read it when it does.
- **Read the whole framework, not only the dictionary.** Options come from every page that provides the key: hubs, concepts, and specs as well as the filled-in dictionary. When the framework covers something the user's dictionary is missing, such as a Google campaign type, offer it and say it isn't in their dictionary yet.
- **Ask for the pattern, not every value.** Where a value carries a number, register the pattern once: `OFFXX` for a percent-off offer, filled in as `OFF34` when a 34 percent sale runs. Never ask for every amount up front.
- **Describe options; don't invent reasons.** Each option says what it is, from its dictionary description. A reason it suits this piece of work appears only if the user said it or a brief records it. "Purchases (`PUR`): optimizes for completed purchases" is a description. "Purchases, the core of a sale" is a guess.
- **One-off changes stay one-off.** When the user wants something different for one piece of work, such as another naming pattern for one campaign, apply it to that work only. To change the standard itself, ask: "Update your campaign naming conventions so every future campaign uses this? Reply YES to update." Only YES updates the document. Anything else leaves the standard as it is.
- **Permissions are out of scope.** Never ask who owns a folder or a tool, who can open it, or who adds teammates. That is managed in the user's own tools. The [owner](/glossary#owner) of a piece of work is a different thing and stays in scope.

## If you are the agent

This page is your instruction set for every command and skill in this framework. Hold to the contract above, and:

- Route the request through the [router](/mcp-router), then open that page's MCP wiring block: Triggers, Asks in order, Uses, Hands off to, and Output. A skill page carries the same in its runbook.
- Find pages by capability key, never by page name. A Uses or Options key matches every page that provides it or a key below it, as the [registry](/mcp-registry#how-discovery-works) describes, so pages added after this one are found the same way.
- Rank every option list against the context before offering it: top five with a reason each, then "or see all".
- Pull everything the wiring lists before drafting, in one pass, without narrating the reading. Check the matching library for reusable work before making new, and file the output where the wiring's Output says.
- A missing input is one precise ask. A value missing from a dictionary is a question, never a guess.
- Keep every turn under three sentences until the work itself.
- Deliver into the user's recorded locations and summarize in chat in a line or two.
