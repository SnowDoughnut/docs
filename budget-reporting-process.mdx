Budget reporting answers three questions every period: what did we spend, are we on plan, and did the spend do anything. It reads from the same [flat file](/flat-file-template) as every other report, so the spend figure in a budget report and the spend figure in a performance report cannot disagree.

## What each period covers

| Period | Reports | Sits in |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Weekly | Spend to date, [pace](/budget-pacing), cost per result, changes made | [Weekly report](/weekly-report-template) |
| Monthly | The above, plus channel split against plan and reserve status | [Monthly report](/monthly-report-template) |
| Quarterly | Total against approved budget, forecast to year end | [Quarterly report](/quarterly-report-template) |
| Annual | Spend against the year's plan, and what the plan got wrong | [Annual report](/annual-report-template) |

Cadence and ownership are set in [reporting cadence](/reporting-cadence). Each period is assembled from the one below it, not re-derived. See [reporting best practices](/reporting-best-practices).

## What a budget report contains

| Field | Source |
| :-- | :-- |
| Budget approved | The brief in the [campaigns library](/campaign-library-overview) |
| Spend this period | [Flat file](/flat-file-template) |
| Spend to date | Flat file, cumulative |
| Pace | [Calculated](/budget-pacing) against the daily rate |
| Forecast at flight end | Daily rate to date × days remaining, plus spend to date |
| Cost per primary result | Defined in the [data dictionary](/marketing-reporting-data-dictionary) |
| Changes made this period | The change log |
| Reserve status | Untouched, released, or spent |

## The process

<Steps>
  <Step title="1. Pull spend from the flat file">
    Never from a platform dashboard. Platform figures move after the fact as billing reconciles, and a report built from them disagrees with itself a week later.
  </Step>
  <Step title="2. Reconcile against the approved budget">
    Compare against the figure recorded on the brief, not against what people remember agreeing. If they differ, the budget was changed without being recorded, which is the finding.
  </Step>
  <Step title="3. Calculate pace and forecast">
    Both from [budget pacing](/budget-pacing). The forecast is the number people act on, since it says what will happen rather than what has.
  </Step>
  <Step title="4. Read the change log before writing anything">
    Most variances have a recorded cause. A budget shifted between channels on Tuesday explains Wednesday's movement, and reporting it as a performance change is simply wrong.
  </Step>
  <Step title="5. State the variance, then the cause, separately">
    _Blurred:_ "Spend was 12% under because LinkedIn underperformed."

    _Separated:_ "Spend finished 12% under plan. LinkedIn delivered 60% of its allocated budget; the audience was smaller than the platform estimate suggested."

    The reader can then disagree with your explanation without disputing your number.
  </Step>
  <Step title="6. File it">
    Into the archive for that period. The archive is what makes the next period assemblable without re-deriving anything.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Reporting an underspend honestly

Underspend gets softened more often than overspend, because it feels like the safer error. It usually isn't: it means a plan was wrong, or a campaign couldn't deliver, and both are worth knowing.

Report the amount, whether the cause was delivery or a deliberate decision, and whether the money was reallocated or simply not spent. "Came in under budget" without those three is not a finding.

## Exceptions

A campaign still in flight at period end reports spend to date and forecast, not a final figure. Mark it as in-flight so a later report showing a different total doesn't read as a contradiction.
