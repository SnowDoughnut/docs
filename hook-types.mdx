A hook is the first line of an ad or the first seconds of a video: the part that earns the rest a read. Every hook executes an [angle](/angle-concepts); the angle says why this person should care, and the hook is one way of opening that argument. This page is the closed list of ways.

A hook type is a family, not a finished line. "Scarcity" is the type; "Only 40 onboarding slots left this quarter" is the hook. Drafted hooks live in the [angle brief](/angle-brief), tagged with their type, so the next writer starts from an approved line rather than a blank page.

## The types

| Type | What it does | Example |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| FOMO | Points at what others are already getting | The bakeries that switched this quarter are already outselling you |
| Scarcity | Limited supply, real and countable | Only 40 onboarding slots left this quarter |
| Urgency | A real deadline | Launch pricing ends Friday |
| Social proof | Others already trust it | 3,500 bakeries run on Doughnut Labs |
| Value | Price, discount, or saving up front | Full doughnut automation from $19 a month |
| Risk reversal | Removes the hesitation | Try it for 30 days, keep the doughnuts either way |
| Curiosity | Opens a gap the reader wants closed | The one setting most bakeries never change |
| Problem call-out | Names the pain in the reader's words | Still counting trays by hand? |
| Bold result | A specific, provable number | Batch waste down 38% in eight weeks |
| Question | Puts the reader in the scene | What would you do with ten free hours a week? |

These are the same families as the [headline buckets](/skill-google-ad-copy#headline-buckets) in the Google ad copy skill, applied there to a pool of headlines instead of one opening line.

## Picking one

One hook per ad. An ad that opens with scarcity and pivots to social proof mid-sentence spends its strongest real estate saying two half-things. Variants of an ad are different hooks on the same angle, which is also what makes a test readable: when the FOMO variant beats the value variant, you learned something about the audience.

The angle narrows the list. A proof-heavy angle feeds social proof and bold result hooks; an offer angle feeds value, urgency, and scarcity. Check the angle brief's drafted hooks before writing fresh ones.

Two guardrails hold whatever the type:

- A bold result or social proof hook needs a number someone can stand behind, from the approved claims table in [messaging](/messaging).
- Scarcity and urgency have to be true. A countdown that resets is the kind of claim [legal review](/legal-review-process) exists to catch.
