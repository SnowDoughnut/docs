Organic social carries the brand more often than any other channel, at higher volume and with less review. The documents that govern it are the same ones that govern everything else.

## What's different about organic

| | Paid | Organic |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Volume of review | Every ad is briefed and approved | Most posts are not |
| Targeting | A named customer profile | Whoever follows you |
| Failure mode | Wasted spend | Voice drift |
| Measurement | Attributed to a campaign | Only measurable if links are tagged |

Voice drift is the real risk. Nobody sets out to sound different; it happens one unreviewed post at a time. The tone-by-channel matrix in [brand voice and tone](/brand-voice-and-tone) is where a team decides how much personality organic gets, so the answer exists before someone has to improvise it.

## Tagging organic links

Any link in a bio, a post, or a story needs tagging from [UTM standards](/utm-standards), or the traffic lands in reporting as direct and the channel looks like it does nothing.

## Content calendar

Posts are planned in the [**content calendar**](/content-calendar), which lives in your scheduling tool or database and is linked from that page rather than held here.

- [**Content calendar**](/content-calendar) The standard: where it lives, the columns, the statuses.
- [**Planning the content calendar**](/content-calendar-process) The recurring session that fills it, and how content is sequenced around a campaign flight.
- [**Content calendar concepts**](/content-calendar-concepts) Why a row records what it is anchored to, not just a date.

Every row references a message pillar from [messaging](/messaging) rather than being planned as a standalone idea, for the same reason campaigns do.
