_This page is a workbook. Fill it in top to bottom, and expand a concept review if you want the reasoning behind a section. Grayed_ `E.g.` _text is a placeholder. Delete it and replace it with your own._

_Examples on this page use a bullion dealer. Its customers do three things with it: buy metal, sell metal back, and pay to store metal. So its products are "Buy from us", "Sell to us", and "Storage"._

<Note>
  The goal is to name what you sell, once. Campaign names, briefs, and reports then use those names instead of inventing their own.
</Note>

## 1. What you sell

**What counts as one product?** Pick the level at which you would move budget between things.

_E.g. Something a customer comes to us to do: buy metal, sell metal, or store metal. Individual coins and bars are not products here. They are stock, and they change daily._

<Accordion title="Concept review: What you sell">
  The unit you pick here decides how coarse or fine every downstream report can be. List too few and you can never tell which one carried the spend. List every item you stock and the catalog becomes a second copy of your inventory system that nobody maintains.

  Most teams land on the level at which they would actually reallocate budget. If nobody would ever move money between two things, they probably do not need to be two rows. If someone would ask "what did we spend on that one?" and expect an answer, it needs its own row.

  A product is not always a physical thing. For a service business, the products are often the jobs a customer hires you for. The bullion dealer sells metal, but it advertises three different jobs, and each one has its own customers, its own ads, and its own results.
</Accordion>

## 2. Product list

One row per product. The short form is what campaign names and reports carry, so keep it short and never change it.

Type is Product or Offer. An offer is a way of selling a product, such as a discount or free delivery. It attaches to a product and gets its own row so it can be reported on separately.

| Name | Short form | Type | What it is | Status | Owner |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| _E.g. Buy from us_ | _E.g._ `BUY` | _E.g. Product_ | _E.g. Customers buy coins and bars, delivered or collected_ | _E.g. Live_ | _E.g. Sales lead_ |
| _E.g. Sell to us_ | _E.g._ `SELL` | _E.g. Product_ | _E.g. Customers sell metal back to us at a quoted price_ | _E.g. Live_ | _E.g. Buyback lead_ |
| _E.g. Storage_ | _E.g._ `STORE` | _E.g. Product_ | _E.g. Insured vault storage, billed yearly_ | _E.g. Live_ | _E.g. Operations lead_ |
| _E.g. Free delivery over $5,000_ | _E.g._ `FREEDEL` | _E.g. Offer_ | _E.g. Applies to Buy from us_ | _E.g. Live_ | _E.g. Sales lead_ |
| *Add a row* |  |  |  |  |  |

**Status values:** _E.g. Live or Retired. A retired product keeps its row and its short form, so old reports still make sense._

<Warning>
  Never reuse or rename a short form. A retired product's short form stays reserved, because campaigns, exports, and dashboards from its lifetime still carry it. Add a new one instead.
</Warning>

<Accordion title="Concept review: Product list">
  The short form turns a name someone typed into a value a report can group by. Without one, "which products did we spend on last quarter?" is answered by reading campaign names and guessing, which is why the question usually goes unanswered.

  Short matters more than readable. The short form sits inside names that already carry channel, objective, and funnel position, and platforms limit how long those names can be. Teams that start with `sell-your-gold-to-us` end up cutting it down in different ways within a quarter.

  Keeping retired products on the list is the part most teams skip and later regret. The row costs nothing, and it is the only thing that makes a two-year-old report readable after the product is gone.
</Accordion>

## 3. Where the short form goes

**Which names carry the product short form?**

| Level | Carries the short form? |
| :-- | :-- |
| Campaign | _E.g. Yes. Added to the Product table in [campaign naming conventions](/campaign-naming-conventions)._ |
| Ad group or ad set | _E.g. Only where one campaign covers several products._ |
| Ad | _E.g. No. The ad's offer field covers what changes at that level._ |
| *Add a row* |  |

_E.g. A Search campaign for Sell to us carries_ `SELL` _in its product field. The flat file's_ [`product` column](/flat-file-template#dimension-columns) _picks it up from there, so reports can total spend by product._

<Accordion title="Concept review: Where the short form goes">
  A product list nobody puts into names is a reference document. A short form carried in campaign names and split out into the flat file's `product` column is something every report can group by. Both halves are needed: the name carries the value, and the column receives it.

  Campaign level is the usual answer, because most teams already separate products at that level, and `utm_campaign` inherits it for free. Putting it at every level repeats the same value and uses up character limits.
</Accordion>

## 4. Adding and retiring a product

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add it before it is briefed">
    A new product gets its row and its short form in section 2 before any campaign brief mentions it. A value invented at build time is one no report recognizes.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the short form to the naming conventions">
    Add the value to the Product table in [campaign naming conventions](/campaign-naming-conventions), so it is available the first time someone builds a name.
  </Step>
  <Step title="On retirement, change the status only">
    Set the status to Retired. Keep the row and the short form.
  </Step>
</Steps>
