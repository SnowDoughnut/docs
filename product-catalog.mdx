_Fill in one page per product. Grayed_ `E.g.` _text is a placeholder. Delete it and replace it with your own._

_Examples on this page use a bullion dealer. Its customers do three things with it: buy metal, sell metal back, and pay to store metal. So its products are "Buy from us", "Sell to us", and "Storage"._

## Before you start

Decide these once, for every product.

- **What counts as one product.** Pick the level at which you would move budget between things. _E.g. Something a customer comes to us to do. Individual coins and bars are stock, not products._
- **How short forms work.** Each product gets a short form, such as `SELL`. Keep it short, and never rename or reuse it, even after the product is retired. Add it to the Product table in [campaign naming conventions](/campaign-naming-conventions) before any brief mentions the product.
- **Where the short form goes.** Campaign names carry it. Ad set names carry it only where one campaign covers several products. Ads carry the offer's short form instead. The flat file's [`product` column](/flat-file-template#dimension-columns) picks it up from the campaign name.
- **Retiring a product.** Set its status to Retired. Keep the page and the short form, so old campaigns and reports still make sense.

<Accordion title="Concept review: Short forms">
  The short form turns a name someone typed into a value a report can group by. Without one, "which products did we spend on last quarter?" is answered by reading campaign names and guessing.

  Short matters more than readable. The short form sits inside names that already carry channel, objective, and funnel position, and platforms limit how long those names can be.

  Keeping retired products is the part most teams skip and later regret. The page costs nothing, and it is the only thing that makes a two-year-old report readable after the product is gone.
</Accordion>

## Product details

Every product page records these fields alongside the sections below:

- **Product name** and **short form**.
- **Status**: Live, Beta, Sunsetting, or Retired.
- **Owner**: who keeps the page up to date.
- **Tags**: any grouping you report on, such as product line or category.
- **Price** and **SKU**.
- **Destination URL** and **asset folder**.
- **Shopping feeds**: which feeds list this product, such as [Google Merchant Center](/google-merchant-center-overview) or [Meta Commerce](/meta-commerce-overview). Leave it empty if you don't run one.

## 1. One-line description

**What is it, in one sentence a new hire would understand?**

_E.g. Customers sell metal back to us at a quoted price, paid the same day._

## 2. Long description

**What does the customer get, how does it work, and what makes it different?**

_E.g. Customers bring or post coins and bars. We test them, quote against the live spot price, and pay by bank transfer once they accept. No minimum amount._

## 3. Who it's for

**Which customer profiles buy it?** Link the profiles from the [customer profile library](/customer-profile-library-overview) rather than describing the customer again.

_E.g. Inherited-metal sellers; collectors trimming a collection._

## 4. Price and offer

**What does it cost, and how is it charged?**

_E.g. We pay spot minus 2%. No fees._

Link any live offers for this product. See [Offers](#offers).

## 5. Where it lives

**Where do ads send people, and where are its approved files?** Record the destination URL and the asset folder.

_E.g._ `snowdoughnut.com/sell` _and the "Sell to us" folder in the brand drive._

## Optional

### 6. Approved claims

**Which claims about this product are approved, word for word?** Include any required qualifier.

_E.g. "Paid the same day", only for offers accepted before 2 p.m._

### 7. Proof points

**What backs the claims up?** Numbers, reviews, awards, or case studies, each with its source.

_E.g. 4.8 stars from 2,300 reviews on Trustpilot, September 2026._

## Offers

An offer is a way of selling a product, such as a discount, a bundle, or free delivery. It has an end date; the product carries on.

Give each offer its own entry: a name, a short form, a status, the discount or terms, start and end dates, the products it applies to, and the campaigns that run it. Ad names carry the offer's short form. See [ad naming conventions](/ad-name-conventions).

_E.g. Free delivery over $5,000, short form_ `FREEDEL`_, applies to Buy from us._
