This is for someone joining a team that has already worked through [get started](/overview). If setup isn't done, do that first instead.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Week 1: Learn the shape of it">
    Read [get started](/overview) to see how the knowledge base is organized and what setup the team has already done. Then read the [glossary](/glossary), which is what stops the next four documents reading as jargon.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Week 1: Learn the brand">
    Read [positioning](/positioning) and [messaging](/messaging) to understand what the brand claims, then [brand voice and tone](/brand-voice-and-tone) for how it sounds. These three explain almost every editorial decision you'll see.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Week 1: Learn the conventions">
    Read the [naming conventions overview](/naming-conventions-overview). You don't need to memorize the patterns, only to know they exist and where to find them.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Week 2: Take the path for your role">
    [Paid media onboarding](/paid-media-onboarding) if you'll work in ad platforms. [Brand onboarding](/brand-onboarding) if you'll produce creative or copy.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Week 2: Do a real task">
    Pick the walkthrough that matches your first piece of work: [make a campaign](/make-a-campaign), [write copy](/write-copy), [brief creative](/brief-creative), [write an article](/write-an-article), or [report on results](/report-on-results). Each one tells you which documents to fill, in what order, and where to save the result.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Two habits worth forming early

**Link, don't paste.** If a brief needs the positioning statement, it links to [positioning](/positioning). Pasting it in creates a second copy that quietly goes out of date.

**File the finished thing.** Every completed brief has a library it belongs in. A brief nobody filed is work nobody can learn from.
