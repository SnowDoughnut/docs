Take an organic social post from idea to published, tagged, and logged. This process covers a single post and a recurring series, which share a brief.

Organic posts are made far more often than paid campaigns and with fewer people involved, so this process is shorter than the paid ones. The parts that stay are the parts that go wrong: tagging, approval, and logging.

<Info>
  This process uses the [**organic social post brief**](/organic-social-post-brief), the [**brand voice and tone**](/brand-voice-and-tone) workbook, the [**UTM standards**](/utm-standards), the [**organic social creative specs**](/organic-social-creative-specs), the [**organic social copy template**](/organic-social-copy-template), the [**content calendar**](/content-calendar), and the [**organic social pre-publish checklist**](/organic-social-pre-publish-checklist).
</Info>

## What you'll end up with

- A completed [**organic social post brief**](/organic-social-post-brief) recording what the post is for, who it speaks to, and what it links to.
- A **published post**, on brand, with alt text, and with any link tagged so the traffic is attributable.
- A **row in the [content calendar](/content-calendar)**, with the published URL, so the post can be found, reported on, and reused later.

One person, the [**owner**](/glossary#owner), is accountable for all three.

## 1. Fill in the post brief

Copy the [**organic social post brief**](/organic-social-post-brief). A single post gets its own; a recurring series shares one across its run.

Start with what the post is for and who it speaks to. Most organic posts do not drive a measurable conversion, and naming the job in plain terms is what keeps a post reviewable without forcing a conversion metric onto something that was never going to produce one.

## 2. Check the drives before making anything

Look in the [creative drive](/creative-drive) and the [video drive](/video-drive) before commissioning new work. An approved asset that already fits, or crops to fit, is faster than a brief and has already cleared review.

If nothing fits, run the [creative brief process](/creative-brief-process). Put the production time on the calendar rather than assuming organic work happens instantly.

## 3. Produce the creative and write the caption

Build to the [organic social creative specs](/organic-social-creative-specs). Organic creative is held to the same brand standard as paid, and most ratios are shared, but two constraints are organic-only: the profile grid re-crops the post centred at a ratio you do not control, and a published post cannot be edited into a different image. Both are cheap to design around and expensive to discover afterwards.

Write the caption against the [brand voice and tone](/brand-voice-and-tone) workbook. If the post belongs to a campaign or makes a claim about the product, run it through the [copy brief](/copy-brief) so the claim matches what the rest of the campaign is saying.

The hook is doing most of the work here. With no budget forcing distribution, a post that is not opened is not seen at all.

## 4. Tag the link

If the post links out, tag the URL with [UTMs](/utm-standards) using an organic medium value.

Untagged organic links land in analytics as direct or referral traffic, which is how organic social ends up appearing to drive nothing while quietly driving something.

Decide where the link goes, too. Some platforms suppress reach on posts with an outbound link in the caption, which is why the link often sits in the first comment instead. On Instagram neither is clickable at all, so the real choices are the bio link and a Story link sticker. The [copy template](/organic-social-copy-template#link-placement) lists what is clickable where. Record the choice in the brief so it is consistent across the series.

## 5. Get approval

Get whatever review the post needs before it is scheduled: [brand](/brand-approval-process), [legal](/legal-review-process), or both, per the [approval process](/approval-process).

A post making a product claim, quoting a customer, or naming a competitor needs the same review a paid ad would. The distribution is different; the exposure is not.

## 6. Run the pre-publish checklist

Run the [**organic social pre-publish checklist**](/organic-social-pre-publish-checklist).

## 7. Publish and log it

Publish, then do the two parts that are easy to skip:

- Paste the published URL into the brief.
- Fill in the [content calendar](/content-calendar) row: status to Published, and the URL.

An unlogged post cannot be reported on, cannot be found again, and cannot be reused. It is the organic equivalent of an article with no register row.

## 8. Check whether it should become paid creative

Review performance after about a week. A post that performed well organically is proven creative you already own, tested against a real audience with no budget behind it.

If it is worth running as paid, arrange the authorization while the post is fresh:

- **TikTok Spark Ads** need an authorization code with an expiry date. Record both in the [TikTok campaign brief](/tiktok-campaign-brief).
- **Meta and LinkedIn** can promote an existing post or run it through creator allowlisting, which needs the account holder's permission.

## Exceptions

- **The post is a reactive or timely reply.** Community management replies and reactive posts do not need a brief. They still follow the brand voice and the approval rules for claims.
- **The post is part of an approved recurring series.** One brief covers the series. Each post still gets its own [content calendar](/content-calendar) row and its own link tagging, because each needs its own URL and status.
- **The post is one of a set supporting a campaign flight.** The set has an order and each piece is timed against the flight. Sequence them in the calendar first, per [planning the content calendar](/content-calendar-process#sequencing-content-around-a-campaign), so they cannot publish out of order.
- **The post is a creator's, not yours.** The creator's own post follows their agreement, not this process. If you plan to run it as paid, the authorization is what you need to record.
