Approval is the step most often improvised under deadline, which is why it needs to be written down before the deadline. This page holds the general workflow. The two specialist reviews have their own pages: [brand approval](/brand-approval-process) and [legal review](/legal-review-process).

## What needs approval

Not everything does. Approving everything is how approval becomes a rubber stamp, and how the things that genuinely need scrutiny get the same three seconds as a social caption.

| Work | Needs | Doesn't need |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| A new campaign concept | Brand, and legal if it makes a claim | A second marketing opinion |
| An ad using existing approved assets and cleared claims | Nothing beyond the brief owner | Brand re-review |
| Anything with a price, a comparison, or a statistic | [Legal review](/legal-review-process) | |
| A new visual treatment or logo application | [Brand approval](/brand-approval-process) | |
| A copy variant inside an approved message pillar | Brief owner | Brand re-review |
| Anything a partner, creator, or customer will publish | Brand and legal | |
| \[add row\] | | |

The pattern worth keeping: approve the *thing that is new*. Reusing cleared claims and approved assets inside an existing brief is production, not a new decision.

## Who approves what

| Review | Approver | Backup | Turnaround |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Brief sign-off | _E.g. Marketing lead_ | _E.g. Head of growth_ | _E.g. 1 working day_ |
| [Brand](/brand-approval-process) | _E.g. Brand lead_ | _E.g. Design lead_ | _E.g. 2 working days_ |
| [Legal](/legal-review-process) | _E.g. External counsel_ | _E.g. None, blocks_ | _E.g. 3 working days_ |
| Budget | _E.g. Head of growth_ | _E.g. Finance_ | _E.g. 1 working day_ |
| Final ship | _E.g. Campaign owner_ | _E.g. Marketing lead_ | _E.g. Same day_ |

A named backup matters more than it looks. A single approver with no backup means every holiday is a freeze.

## The order

<Steps>
  <Step title="1. Approve the brief, not the output">
    The cheapest approval is on the brief, before anything is produced. A concept rejected at brief stage costs a conversation. The same concept rejected after production costs the production.
  </Step>
  <Step title="2. Produce">
    No approvals happen during production. If the brief changes mid-flight, the brief gets updated and re-approved, rather than the change being carried in someone's memory.
  </Step>
  <Step title="3. Run the specialist reviews in parallel">
    Brand and legal review the same version at the same time. Running them in sequence doubles the wait for no benefit, since they check different things.
  </Step>
  <Step title="4. Consolidate feedback into one round">
    One person collects every reviewer's notes, resolves contradictions between them, and sends a single set back. Reviewers do not brief the producer directly.
  </Step>
  <Step title="5. Final ship approval">
    One named person confirms the final version is the approved version. This is the step that catches the file that was revised after approval.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Revision rounds

Agree the number in the brief. Two rounds is a common default: one substantive, one for corrections.

Rounds are consumed by feedback, not by time. Work that comes back with contradictory notes from three reviewers has not used a round; it has failed step 4.

## When an approver doesn't respond

Silence is the most common failure in any approval process, and it needs a stated answer rather than a norm people guess at.

_E.g. If a reviewer doesn't respond within the turnaround above, the campaign owner escalates to the backup. If the backup doesn't respond, brand and budget approvals default to approved and the campaign owner notes the exception on the brief. Legal review never defaults to approved._

## What gets recorded

- Who approved, and which version they approved.
- The date.
- Any condition attached to the approval.
- Any exception taken, and why.

Record it on the brief in the relevant library, not in a message thread. The question "was this approved?" arrives months later, from someone who wasn't there.

## Exceptions

Urgent work still goes through the same reviews, compressed. What changes is the turnaround, not the list of reviewers. Skipping legal because something is urgent is the case that costs the most.
