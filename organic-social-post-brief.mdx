_This page is a workbook. Fill it in before the post is made, not after. Each section has a "Concept review" dropdown explaining what the section is for._

<Note>
  Organic social posts are cheaper to make than paid ads and are made far more often, so this brief is deliberately shorter than the paid platform briefs. Fill in the core for every post. A recurring series can share one brief across its whole run.

  Grayed `E.g.` text is a placeholder. Examples use **Doughnut Labs**, a SaaS company that sells disruptive Doughnut Technology.
</Note>

## Setup

| Field | Fill in |
| :-- | :-- |
| [Owner](/glossary#owner) | _E.g. Marco Silva_ |
| Channel or channels | _E.g. LinkedIn and Instagram_ |
| Format | _E.g. Carousel_ |
| One post or a series | _E.g. Series of four, one per week_ |
| [Marketing campaign brief](/marketing-campaign-brief), if any | _E.g. [Fresh Batch spring trial push](https://link)_ |
| Publish date and time | _E.g. 22 Apr, 09:00_ |
| Anchored to | _E.g. Google flight launch, 22 Apr_ |
| Position in the sequence, if part of a set | _E.g. 2 of 3, mid-flight_ |
| [Content calendar](/content-calendar) row | _E.g. \[link\]_ |

<Accordion title="Concept review: Setup">
  Not every organic post belongs to a campaign, and the link field is allowed to be empty. Posts that do belong to one should say so, because that is what lets the campaign's retrospective account for organic reach alongside paid.

  Recording the format up front matters more on organic than it looks. A carousel, a short video, and a text post are three different production jobs with three different lead times, and picking the format at the point of writing the caption usually means picking whichever one is fastest.

  The anchor and sequence rows exist because a set of posts supporting a campaign is a single argument delivered in parts. A date alone says when a post goes out; an anchor says what it was timed against, which is what lets the whole set move together when a flight slips. Posts published out of sequence read as non sequiturs, since each one was written assuming the one before it had landed.
</Accordion>

## Objective and customer profile

| Field | Fill in |
| :-- | :-- |
| What this post is for | _E.g. Show the one-afternoon setup, so cold followers stop assuming a long implementation_ |
| Reader action | _E.g. Saves the post, or clicks through to the setup guide_ |
| [Customer profile](/customer-profile-library-overview) | _E.g. [Mid-market RevOps lead](https://link)_ |
| Where it sits in the funnel | _E.g. Top of funnel, awareness_ |

<Accordion title="Concept review: Objective and customer profile">
  Most organic posts do not drive a measurable conversion, and pretending otherwise leads to bad decisions in both directions: killing posts that were building familiarity, and defending posts that were doing nothing.

  Naming the job the post is meant to do, in plain terms, is what makes it reviewable without forcing a conversion metric onto something that was never going to produce one.
</Accordion>

## Hook and copy

| Field | Fill in |
| :-- | :-- |
| Hook, first line or first frame | _E.g. Most reporting rollouts take a quarter. Here is one that took an afternoon._ |
| Caption | _E.g. \[full caption text\], written to the [platform limit](/organic-social-copy-template#character-limits)_ |
| Call to action | _E.g. Full walkthrough in the comments_ |
| Hashtags or keywords | _E.g. Three topical tags, no branded tag_ |
| [Copy brief](/copy-brief), if one exists | _E.g. [COPY\_fresh-batch-organic\_2026-04-02\_v01](https://link)_ |

<Accordion title="Concept review: Hook and copy">
  On organic social the hook is doing almost all of the work, because there is no budget forcing distribution. A post that is not opened is not seen at all, which is a harsher version of the same problem paid creative has.

  A short post does not need a full [copy brief](/copy-brief). A campaign-linked post, or one making a claim about the product, usually does, for the same reason a paid ad does: the claim has to match what the rest of the campaign is saying and what the company can actually support.
</Accordion>

## Creative

| Field | Fill in |
| :-- | :-- |
| What is needed | _E.g. Four carousel cards, 1080 × 1350_ |
| [Creative brief](/creative-brief), if new work | _E.g. [Fresh Batch organic carousel brief](https://link)_ |
| Existing asset, if reused | _E.g. [Setup timeline graphic](https://link) from the [creative drive](/creative-drive)_ |
| Specs followed | _E.g. [Organic social creative specs](/organic-social-creative-specs), Meta ratios_ |
| Survives the profile grid crop | _E.g. Yes, subject centred, checked in preview_ |
| Accessibility | _E.g. Alt text written for each card. Captions burned into video_ |

<Accordion title="Concept review: Creative">
  Organic and paid creative are held to the same brand standard and the same specs, even though organic tends to be produced faster and with fewer approvals. A post that is off-brand is off-brand regardless of whether money was spent on it.

  Alt text and captions belong in the brief rather than being remembered at upload. They are the two things most often skipped when a post is published in a hurry, and both are part of the post reaching the people it was made for.
</Accordion>

## Link and tracking

| Field | Fill in |
| :-- | :-- |
| Link, if any | _E.g. [doughnutlabs.com/setup-guide](https://link)_ |
| Where the link goes | _E.g. Bio link on Instagram; first comment on LinkedIn. See [link placement](/organic-social-copy-template#link-placement) for what is clickable where_ |
| [UTMs](/utm-standards) | _E.g._ `?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=organic_social&utm_campaign=FBS` _. Organic social has no platform campaign object, so_ `utm_campaign` _carries the marketing campaign short form from the [naming dictionary](/campaign-naming-conventions), not a fresh slug. See [channels with no platform campaign](/utm-standards#channels-with-no-platform-campaign)._ |

<Warning>
  Tag organic links with UTMs the same way paid links are tagged, using an organic medium value. Untagged organic traffic lands in analytics as direct or referral, which is how organic social ends up looking like it drives nothing.
</Warning>

## Post-publish

| Field | Fill in |
| :-- | :-- |
| [Content calendar](/content-calendar) row updated | _E.g. Status Published, URL filled in, 22 Apr_ |
| Worth boosting as paid | _E.g. Review after 7 days_ |
| Available for Spark Ads or allowlisting | _E.g. Yes, authorization code recorded in the [TikTok campaign brief](/tiktok-campaign-brief)_ |

<Accordion title="Concept review: Post-publish">
  A strong organic post is the cheapest source of proven paid creative you have, because it has already been tested against a real audience with no budget behind it. Checking for that deliberately, rather than noticing by accident, is what turns organic into an input for paid rather than a parallel activity.

  If a post is going to run as a Spark Ad or through creator allowlisting, the authorization has to be arranged while the post is fresh and the relationship is live. See the [TikTok campaign brief](/tiktok-campaign-brief) for what needs recording.
</Accordion>
