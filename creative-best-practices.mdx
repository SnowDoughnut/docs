Applied guidance for anyone briefing or producing to a [creative brief](/creative-brief). The reasoning behind the brief's sections is in [creative brief concepts](/creative-brief-concepts).

## Brief the problem, not the picture

A brief that describes a layout has already made the design decisions, badly, and leaves a designer executing someone else's rough idea. A brief that names the objective, the reader, the one message, and the constraints leaves room for a better answer than the briefer had.

The test: could two good designers read this brief and produce two different, both-correct answers? If only one answer is possible, the brief is a spec.

## Design for the smallest, most cropped placement

Most creative is approved at full size on a large screen and runs at a third of that, cropped square, muted, on a phone. The version that matters is the worst one.

- Check the logo is above minimum size in the smallest placement, per [logo usage](/logo-usage).
- Check text contrast at the worst spot over the image, not the average, against the pairings in [visual identity](/visual-identity).
- Check the square and vertical crops before approving the master.
- Assume sound is off. Video that needs audio to make sense makes no sense.

## One idea per asset

An asset carrying three messages carries none. If the campaign has three things to say, that's three assets, or a sequence, not one crowded frame. The message comes from a single pillar in [messaging](/messaging).

## Check what exists before commissioning

The [asset directory](/asset-directory) indexes the creative drive, video drive, and landing pages. An approved asset that already exists, or crops to fit, is faster than a brief and has already cleared review. Briefing new work when a usable asset exists is the most common avoidable cost in creative production.

## Batch feedback into rounds

Feedback sent as it occurs to people produces a moving target and burns goodwill. Collect it, resolve internal disagreements before sending, and cite the brief line each request comes from.

If a request isn't in the brief but genuinely matters, add it to the brief first. That makes the scope change visible rather than remembered differently by each side.

## Name and file it at delivery, not later

The moment an asset is approved is the only moment anyone is motivated to file it properly. Name to [file naming conventions](/file-naming-conventions), save where the [asset directory](/asset-directory) says, record rights and expiry in [brand assets](/brand-assets), and link the finished files from the brief in the [creative brief library](/creative-brief-library).

Rights especially: a stock license or a customer's likeness release has an expiry that the file itself gives no sign of.

## The review failures that cost rounds

| Failure | Cost |
| :-- | :-- |
| No brief, or a brief filled in after the work | Nothing to review against, so review becomes taste |
| Reviewing the master, not the crop | Approved creative fails at the placement that runs |
| Feedback as taste, not as brief lines | Endless rounds, no convergence |
| Scope added without updating the brief | Two versions of what was agreed |
| Approving before specs are checked | Rebuild after the campaign is built |
| Files left in a message thread | The asset is unfindable in a month |
