The copy drive holds approved copy that has run: ad copy by platform and format, email copy, landing page copy, and scripts.

Check here before writing new copy. A line that has already run has performance behind it and has already cleared review, which is two advantages a fresh draft does not have.

The approved claims folder earns its place. It is the list of things the company can say about the product without a fresh legal review, and it is what a writer should check before making a claim rather than after.

## Naming

Every file follows the [file naming conventions](/file-naming-conventions), with a `COPY` prefix:

`COPY_q3-trial-push_2027-08-20_v01_APPR.md`

The descriptor should match the campaign or asset the copy was written for, so a line in a live ad can be traced back to the document it came from.

## Before you reuse a line

- **Check it is still true.** Product claims go stale. A line about a feature that has changed is worse than no line.
- **Check the claim is still approved.** A claim cleared once is not cleared forever, particularly where it involves a number, a comparison, or a customer.
- **Check the character limits for the new channel.** A headline that fits Meta will not fit a Google Search headline. See the channel [copy templates](/ad-copy-standards).
- **Check the customer profile matches.** A line written to overcome one profile's objection often means nothing to another.

<Warning>
  Copy carrying a statistic, a customer quote, or a competitive comparison needs its source checked before it runs again. The number that was current when the line was written may not be current now, and the claim travels further than the evidence behind it.
</Warning>
