An angle is the reason your product matters to a particular person, stated as one proposition they could agree or disagree with.

"You can turn the nights you never use at your holiday home into a holiday somewhere else" is an angle. "Book now" is not. Neither is "we have great inventory." An angle makes a claim specific enough that someone can recognize themselves in it or decide it is not for them.

Angles are what creative and copy get built from. This page explains the idea. The fill-in workbook is the [angle brief](/angle-brief), and every angle you are running lives in the [angle library](/angle-library).

## Angle type, angle, and hook are three different things

These three get used interchangeably, and the distinction is what makes an angle worth writing down.

| Level | What it is | Example | How often it changes |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Angle type | The kind of argument being made | _Outcome_ | Fixed. A closed list in the [naming dictionary](/ad-name-conventions#message-angle) |
| Angle | The specific proposition | _The four hours you spend rebuilding the Monday report could be spent reading it_ | Slowly. Monthly at most |
| Hook | One execution of it in a first line or frame | _"It's 9am Monday. Your report is already done."_ | Constantly. Weekly, per creative |

One angle produces many hooks. That is the whole reason a library works: the hook is what you swap when a creative tires, and the angle is what you keep when something works.

Collapsing the two costs you the accumulation. If an angle and a hook are the same thing, then a hook that underperformed retires the argument underneath it, and next quarter someone proposes the same argument again with no record that it was ever tried.

<Note>
  The [message angle dictionary](/ad-name-conventions#message-angle) in the ad naming conventions owns the type values and their short forms, because those are what a report groups by. This library owns the angles themselves. Each angle in the library carries one type.
</Note>

## Why an account needs several angles at once

A single angle reaches the people who already agree with its premise. Everyone whose reason for buying is different never sees an argument aimed at them, and no amount of budget on the same argument changes that.

This is what creative diversity means in practice, and platforms now reward it directly. Meta's delivery systems treat genuinely different creative as separate opportunities to find an audience, and treat near-duplicates as the same creative. Reported working ranges sit around six meaningfully different ads in an ad set as a floor, with stronger accounts holding fifteen to twenty active creatives and refreshing a quarter to a third of them each month.

The word carrying the weight is "meaningfully." Five versions of one ad with different background colours is one creative wearing five coats of paint. Five ads built on five different angles is five arguments.

<Info>
  The commonly quoted figure that creative accounts for 56% of sales lift comes from Nielsen and Nielsen Catalina Solutions, from a meta-analysis of around 500 packaged goods campaigns published in 2017. It is often attributed to Meta, which cites it rather than produced it. Treat it as directional evidence that creative is the largest single lever, not as a number that transfers exactly to your account.
</Info>

## What makes two angles genuinely different

Two angles are different when they would persuade different people, or persuade the same person for different reasons. The test is whether someone could find one compelling and the other irrelevant.

| Genuinely different | Cosmetically different |
| :-- | :-- |
| A different reason to care | The same reason, reworded |
| A different belief being overcome | The same claim with a new image |
| A different person recognizing themselves | The same argument in a new format |
| A different emotional payoff | A new headline on the same proposition |

Format is not an angle. Running the same proposition as a static, a video, and a carousel gives you three executions of one argument. That has value for placement coverage. It does not give you creative diversity.

## Angle fatigue is not creative fatigue

The two look identical in a performance chart and have different fixes, which is why the distinction is worth holding.

Creative fatigue is the audience having seen this specific execution too many times. A new hook, a new edit, or a new image resets it, because the argument is still landing and only the delivery has gone stale.

Angle fatigue is the audience having heard this argument too many times. The people it was going to convince have been convinced, and everyone left has already declined it. New visuals on the same proposition do not recover this, and the usual outcome is a team producing creative faster and faster against an argument that has stopped working.

The diagnostic is the library rather than the platform. If every live angle is a variation of one proposition, a declining account has an angle problem no production schedule will fix.

## Where angles come from

An angle sits at the meeting point of two things you already have: something true about the product, and something the customer already believes.

- **The product truth on its own** is a feature. It is accurate and gives nobody a reason to care.
- **The customer belief on its own** is an insight. It describes a person and asks nothing of them.
- **The angle** is the sentence connecting them, where the product truth answers the belief.

That is why the [angle brief](/angle-brief) asks for the customer profile and the belief before it asks for the proposition. An angle written without a person in mind is a product description with better punctuation.

<Note>
  Finding angles is creative work: customer interviews, review mining, sales call notes, and the arguments that already come up when someone explains the product out loud. This framework does not teach that. What it records is the angle you arrived at, so it can be built from, reported on, and found again. See [what this framework covers](/what-this-framework-covers).
</Note>

## What an angle is not

- **A campaign.** Several campaigns can run the same angle, and one campaign usually runs several.
- **An offer.** The offer is what is on the table. The angle is why it matters. A discount is an offer that a discount angle argues for, and the same offer can be argued for on urgency, outcome, or social proof.
- **A customer profile.** The profile is who. The angle is why they should care. One profile supports several angles.
- **A hook or a headline.** Those are executions. See the table at the top.
- **A brand message.** [Messaging](/messaging) is stable and says what the brand stands for. Angles are the arguments you test against it, and they turn over.
