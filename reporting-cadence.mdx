Each cadence answers a different question: the [daily brief](/daily-brief) flags exceptions and pacing, a [weekly report](/weekly-reporting-process) catches what needs attention now, a [monthly report](/monthly-reporting-process) checks the target, a [quarterly review](/quarterly-reporting-process) checks the strategy, and an [annual review](/yearly-reporting-process) sets next year's plan. Every cadence is a rollup of the flat file's dated rows, not a new report; see [data quality and rollup concepts](/data-quality-and-rollup-concepts) for how that works, and [report writing concepts](/report-writing-concepts#cadence-changes-what-a-report-is-for-not-just-its-length) for matching reporting frequency to decision frequency.

<Note>
  Two roles appear in the register. **Drafted by** can be a person or an AI agent connected to the flat file and your sources of truth. **Approved by** is always a person: the report owner reads, edits, and approves before a report goes out. **Auto-send** is that owner's standing rule to skip the per-report approval for a cadence, set here once rather than decided send by send. Pick the channel each audience actually reads; one team wants everything in Slack, another wants email, another wants a doc. A daily brief usually auto-sends, since it reports only exceptions; whether anything else does is yours to decide.
</Note>

## Where sent reports go

Every sent report gets logged in its archive: [weekly](/weekly-report-archive), [monthly](/monthly-report-archive), [quarterly](/quarterly-report-archive), [annual](/annual-report-archive). The daily brief is not archived; its numbers already live in the flat file, and yesterday's brief has no reader.
