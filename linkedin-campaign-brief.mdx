| Awareness | Brand awareness |
| Consideration | Website visits, Engagement, Video views |
| Conversions | Lead generation, Website conversions, Job applicants |

<Warning>
  The objective and the ad format together decide which bid strategies are available. Not every strategy appears for every combination, so confirm the pairing in Campaign Manager before committing a bid approach in this brief.
</Warning>

<Accordion title="Concept review: Objective and conversion action">
  On LinkedIn the objective narrows both the available ad formats and the available bid strategies, so it is a structural decision made early rather than a label applied at the end.

  There is a real choice between a native lead form and a landing page, and it is a trade rather than a preference. Lead forms convert at a higher rate because they pre-fill from the member's profile, and they produce leads who never saw your page and may be less qualified. Recording which one this campaign uses, and why, is what makes a later argument about lead quality resolvable.
</Accordion>

## Budget and bid strategy

| Field | Fill in |
| :-- | :-- |
| Budget type | _E.g. Daily_ |
| Amount | _E.g. \$300 per day, \$9,000 total_ |
| Bid strategy | _E.g. Cost cap_ |
| Cost cap value | _E.g. \$180 per trial signup_ |
| Why this strategy | _E.g. Known cost per signup from Q1; cap protects it while audience is narrow_ |

LinkedIn's bid strategies, as of 7 August 2026:

| Strategy | What it does | Charged by |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Maximum delivery | Spends the full budget for the most results, no bid input | Impressions |
| Cost cap | Holds cost per key result near a target you set | Impressions |
| Manual bidding | You set the bid for each auction | Varies by objective and format |

<Accordion title="Concept review: Budget and bid strategy">
  LinkedIn costs more per click and per lead than the other paid platforms in this knowledge base, usually by a wide margin. That changes what a sensible test budget looks like: a budget that would produce a clear read on Meta can produce almost no data here, and a campaign that ends "inconclusive" was often just underfunded.

  Maximum delivery gives up cost control in exchange for spending the budget. Cost cap keeps a ceiling and will underdeliver to hold it. Which failure you prefer is the actual decision, and it is easier to make deliberately at brief stage than in week three.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Concept review: Campaign structure">
  On LinkedIn the campaign is the targeting unit, not the ad set, so structure decisions here are really audience decisions. Splitting one audience across several campaigns divides an already small pool and slows every one of them out of learning.

  Audience overlap is the specific risk. Two campaigns targeting job titles that substantially overlap will bid against each other in the same auctions, which raises your own costs. Listing the audiences side by side in one table is the cheapest way to notice that before it happens.
</Accordion>

<Warning>
  Audience expansion and the LinkedIn Audience Network are both on by default in some setups. Both widen delivery beyond the audience you defined. Set each one deliberately and record why, so a later change in cost per lead can be traced to a decision rather than a default.
</Warning>

<Accordion title="Concept review: Targeting">
  LinkedIn's targeting is the reason to be on the platform and the reason it is expensive. Firmographic and job-title targeting reaches people you cannot reliably reach elsewhere, and the auction prices that access accordingly.

  Audience size is worth recording because it sets the ceiling on what the campaign can do. Too narrow and delivery stalls regardless of budget; too broad and the targeting advantage that justified the cost has been given away. The number belongs in the brief so the trade is visible before launch rather than inferred afterward.
</Accordion>
