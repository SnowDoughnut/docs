---

Standing pages are the pages that exist because the company exists: the homepage, pricing, product and feature pages, contact and demo request. They are not built for a campaign and they do not retire when one ends.

This register is where they get an owner, a review date, and a record of what depends on them.

<Note>
  Campaign landing pages are a different object with a different lifecycle, and they live in the [landing page library](/landing-page-library). Use that register for anything built for one campaign or one offer, typically at a `/lp/` URL, that retires when the offer does.
</Note>

## Which register a page belongs in

| | Standing page | Campaign landing page |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Exists because | The company does | A campaign or a standing offer does |
| Retires when | It is replaced or the product changes | The offer ends |
| Offer | Several, or none specific | Exactly one |
| Customer profile | Several | Usually one |
| Built through | Not built through a process; it predates most campaigns | [Building a landing page](/landing-page-process) |
| Register | This page | [Landing page library](/landing-page-library) |

<Info>
  The entry gate on [building a landing page](/landing-page-process) asks for "a reason the page exists: a marketing campaign brief, or a standing offer." That gate governs briefing a **new** page. A standing page does not clear it and does not need to, because nobody is deciding whether to build a homepage. What it needs is the ownership and change control on this page.
</Info>

**What counts as a standing page here:** _E.g. Any page carrying a conversion action or receiving campaign traffic that is not a campaign landing page. Blog posts and articles are covered by the [article library](/article-library-overview) instead._

<Accordion title="Concept review: Why standing pages need a register at all">
  Campaign pages get a register because they multiply. Nobody forgets the homepage exists, so the argument for registering it is different: you need to know who owns it and what breaks when it changes.

  A campaign page usually serves one campaign, so changing it affects one thing. A homepage may be the destination for every brand campaign, several product campaigns, the email footer, and every organic bio link at once. The blast radius is larger and completely invisible from inside the page.

  Review dates do a different job here too. A campaign page is reviewed because its offer expires. A standing page is reviewed because the product moved underneath it: a plan renamed, a claim that is no longer true, a screenshot two versions out of date. Nothing prompts that, which is why it needs a date.
</Accordion>

## Changing a standing page

| The change is | Treat it as |
| :-- | :-- |
| A copy or layout change you expect to improve results, on a page with meaningful traffic | An [experiment](/creating-an-experiment). Control is the current page, and the result decides whether it ships |
| A factual correction: wrong price, expired claim, dead link | An edit. Make it, record it in Last changed, no test needed |
| A change forced by something else: a product rename, a legal requirement | An edit. Record what forced it |
| A rebuild or a new page template | Brief it. Use the [landing page brief](/landing-page-brief), and keep the row here |

<Info>
  The distinction is whether you are testing a belief or correcting a fact. A hero rewrite you think will convert better is a belief, and beliefs about high-traffic pages are worth testing rather than shipping. A price that changed is a fact, and testing it would be absurd.
</Info>

## Reviewing a standing page

At each review date, check:

- The claims are still true and still approved, against [messaging](/messaging).
- Product names and screenshots match what ships today.
- The conversion action still fires, confirmed with a test submission.
- Every link resolves, including the ones in the footer nobody looks at.
- The page still matches the [product catalog](/product-catalog) entry for whatever it sells.
- What points at it, in the table above, is still accurate.
